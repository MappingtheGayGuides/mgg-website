# Introduction to the Damron Travel Guides

The Bob Damron Address Books represent one of the most significant resources for understanding LGBTQ+ spaces and communities in the United States during the latter half of the 20th century. First published in 1964, these travel guides served as essential tools for queer travelers navigating a landscape where same-sex intimacy was criminalized in most states.

## The Historical Context

When Bob Damron published his first guide in 1964, the United States was in the midst of significant social and legal challenges for LGBTQ+ individuals. Most states had laws criminalizing same-sex intimacy, and the Stonewall Riots were still five years away. In this hostile environment, finding safe spaces for community, socializing, and connection was both essential and dangerous.

## What the Guides Contained

Damron's guides included listings for:
- Bars and cocktail lounges
- Bookstores and community centers
- Restaurants and cafes
- Bathhouses and cruising grounds
- Cinemas and entertainment venues
- Hotels and accommodations

Each listing provided essential information about the establishment, including addresses, phone numbers, and often brief descriptions of the clientele or atmosphere.

## The Green Book Parallel

Much like the Green Books that African Americans used to find friendly businesses during the Jim Crow era, Damron's guides helped queer people identify safe spaces in an era of legal and social persecution. These guides were not just travel resources—they were survival tools that connected isolated individuals to broader communities.

## Digital Preservation

Today, the Mapping the Gay Guides project works to preserve and digitize these invaluable historical documents. By making this data accessible through interactive maps and searchable databases, we hope to preserve the history of LGBTQ+ spaces and communities for future generations.

The Damron guides remind us of the resilience and resourcefulness of queer communities in the face of systemic discrimination, and they serve as important historical documents for understanding the evolution of LGBTQ+ rights and spaces in America.
